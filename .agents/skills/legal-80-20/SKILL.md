# Legal 80/20

Legal, accounting, and tax work is overhead. Spend as little time on it as safely possible, and put the rest of your energy into building the business.

The 80/20 rule: use the standard setup, run 1 focused check, decide, sign, and move on.

## Default path

1. **Use the standard setup.** Do what normal tech companies and solo founders in your country already do. Do not invent custom legal structures unless you have a strong reason.
2. **Keep contracts short and plain.** Use standard clauses. Do not add pages of edge-case clauses for scenarios that will probably never happen.
3. **Run 1 focused check.** Do at most 1 research pass per change. Ask one question: does this break a serious law or create a major financial/legal risk?
4. **Decide.** If there is no serious issue, move forward now. If there is one, fix that one thing and proceed.
5. **Sign and move on.** A draft that is good enough to sign gets signed. Stop polishing.

## What counts as serious

Only these are worth stopping for:

- A meaningful unexpected tax bill, penalty, or missed mandatory filing.
- A contractor relationship that looks like disguised employment under local law.
- Losing ownership of your core IP, code, domain, or company assets.
- Unlimited personal liability for the founder or directors.
- Anything unlawful: backdating, invented facts, or fake documents.

Everything else is small. Do not stall on minor wording quirks, small fees, or theoretical edge cases.

## External lawyers and accountants

Involve licensed professionals when it truly matters:

- Mandatory filings, bookkeeping, tax returns, and notarized acts.
- Large or irreversible moves: restructuring, fundraising, selling IP, or high-stakes disputes.
- When your check finds a serious issue you cannot resolve cleanly.

When you do email them, put all your questions into 1 short email right away. Never leave "ask the accountant" sitting as an open todo that nobody sends.

## Output

Lead with the decision and the next action. Mention only issues that would actually change the decision.
